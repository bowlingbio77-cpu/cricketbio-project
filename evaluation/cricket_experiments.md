## 2. Why no composite score

The brief forbids ranking models on arbitrary scores, and this report follows that. This report declares no overall ranking and no single champion: each metric is compared on its own, and a metric is only compared when at least two arms have it **measured**.

## 3. What the run actually showed

Two different things are being measured in this report, and conflating them is the main way this experiment could lie:

- **Selector behaviour is measured.** How often a bowler is confirmed, how often identity switches, and whether the prior changes the chosen track need no labels -- they are read straight off the run, with no reference to what is actually correct.
- **Selector accuracy is `NOT MEASURED`.** Whether the chosen track is *the bowler* needs a human. Until then, behaviour numbers cannot be promoted into a claim of improvement.

| Behaviour | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|
| Clips run | 8 | 8 |
| Bowler auto-confirmed | 1/8 | 4/8 |
| Total identity switches | 9 | 10 |

In 8 of 8 clips the prior selected a **different track** than arm A did.

**Read this correctly.** The prior changed the chosen track in 8 of 8 clips and raised auto-confirmations from 1 to 4. That is *not* evidence that the prior helps. Confirmation is a self-assessment by the selector: a prior that pushes a standing batsman who happens to be low in frame over the line would raise exactly this count while making selection **worse**. The number that would settle it is `bowler_top1_accuracy` against a human, which is `NOT MEASURED` for all four arms.

**A limitation, stated rather than tuned away.** The prior's maximum bonus is `weight x near-ground fraction`, i.e. up to 0.50 on a 0-1 score. Arm A's cricket-evidence scores for these clips sit in roughly the 0.3-0.6 range, so a bonus of that size is large enough to outweigh the evidence term and decide the winner on its own. That is a known weakness of this first arm B, not a bug. The weight is deliberately left alone: shrinking it until the arm stops flipping selections would be threshold tuning against an unlabelled set, which is the exact failure this brief forbids. The next honest step is a human-labelled holdout, then a *pre-registered* weight chosen on the validation split only.
